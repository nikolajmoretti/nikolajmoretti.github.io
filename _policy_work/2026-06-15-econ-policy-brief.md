European economies' heavy reliance on imported fossil fuels exposes households and firms to costly foreign supply shocks. While government relief measures mitigate these impacts, they create two fundamental challenges: a policy trilemma between targeted support, energy-saving incentives, and administrative feasibility; and a dynamic incentive problem where expected bailouts act as implicit insurance, slowing the shift to domestic renewables. For example, Germany’s EUR 55–187 billion gas relief packages during the 2022/23 crisis equate to an implicit subsidy of EUR 15–40/tCO₂ (averaging EUR 18/tCO₂ for gas and EUR 10/tCO₂ for oil across the EU-27). To prevent entrenching fossil fuel dependence, governments should explicitly price this implicit insurance into fossil fuels outside of crises rather than socializing costs during them.
